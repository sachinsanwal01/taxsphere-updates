    Related party — Section 2(76)

    Companies Act, 2013 — applies to every company

    (i)Director / KMP & relatives

    A director, key managerial personnel, or their relative.

    (ii)Firm as partner

    A firm in which a director, manager, or relative of either is a partner.

    (iii)Private company

    A private company in which a director or manager (or their relative) is a member or director.

    (iv)Public company

    A public company in which a director or manager is a director and holds, along with relatives, more than 2% of paid-up share capital.

    (v)Body corporate under instruction

    Any body corporate whose Board, MD or manager is accustomed to act on the directions of a director or manager (excludes advice given in a professional capacity).

    (vi)Person giving instructions

    Any person on whose directions a director or manager is accustomed to act (same professional-advice carve-out).

    (vii)Holding / subsidiary / associate

    A holding, subsidiary or associate company, and a subsidiary of a common holding company (fellow subsidiary).

    (viii)Investing company / venturer

    An investing company or the venturer of the company (as defined in the Accounting Standards).

    (ix)Holding company's director/KMP

    A director or KMP of the holding company, or their relative.

    SEBI LODR — Regulation 2(1)(zc)

    Additional coverage for listed entities — as amended November 2025

  • Any related party under Section 2(76) of the Companies Act, or under the applicable Accounting Standards.
  • Any person or entity forming part of the promoter or promoter group of the listed entity.
  • Any person or entity holding 10% or more of the equity shares of the listed entity, directly or on a beneficial basis, at any time during the immediately preceding financial year.
  • Directors or key managerial personnel of the listed entity or its subsidiary, and their relatives — explicitly captured following the November 2025 amendment.
    • Retail-purchase carve-out (Nov 2025 amendment) Retail purchases by directors or KMP (and their relatives) from the listed entity or its subsidiary are excluded from the definition of an RPT, provided the same terms are uniformly offered to all directors, KMPs and their relatives.

    Contracts requiring approval — Section 188(1)

    Six categories of related-party contracts covered by the section

    CategoryCovers
    Goods / materialsSale, purchase or supply of any goods or materials.
    PropertySelling, disposing of, or buying property of any kind.
    LeasingLeasing of property of any kind.
    ServicesAvailing or rendering of any services.
    Agency appointmentAppointment of a related party's agent for purchase or sale of goods, materials, services or property.
    Office or place of profitAppointment of a related party to any office or place of profit in the company, its subsidiary or associate.
    UnderwritingUnderwriting the subscription of any securities or derivatives of the company.

    The ordinary-course + arm's-length exemption

    A transaction is outside Section 188's approval requirements only if it satisfies both conditions together:

  • It is entered into in the ordinary course of business of the company.
  • It is on an arm's length basis — as if between unrelated parties, with no favourable terms flowing from the relationship.
    • Satisfying only one condition does not exempt the transaction — both must hold. Under SEBI LODR, even transactions meeting both conditions are not exempt from Audit Committee approval or disclosure — the exemption there only narrows the shareholder-approval requirement, not the AC's oversight.

    What "office or place of profit" means

    Any office where the appointee receives remuneration in any form — salary, fees, commission, or the free use of company property (accommodation, car, etc.) that exceeds normal entitlement. The Rule 15 threshold for this category is a monthly remuneration exceeding ₹2.5 lakh.

    Who approves what

    Three layers, each with its own trigger and voting rule

    Board
    • Required for every RPT not exempt under the ordinary-course + arm's-length test.
    • Must be passed by resolution at a Board meeting — not by circular resolution.
    • An interested director cannot be present during discussion or voting on that item.
    Audit Committee
    • Mandatory where the company is required to have an AC — every listed company, and public companies crossing the Section 177 thresholds.
    • Approves, or grants omnibus approval for, all RPTs before they go to the Board.
    • Omnibus approval criteria are laid down by the AC and are valid for a maximum of one year before renewal.
    Shareholders
    • Ordinary resolution required once a Rule 15 threshold is crossed (Companies Act) or a transaction is material (SEBI LODR).
    • Companies Act: related-party members cannot vote, unless 90%+ of members (in number) are relatives of promoters or are themselves related parties.
    • SEBI LODR: no related party may vote on a material RPT resolution — this 90% exception does not apply to listed entities.

    Explanatory statement — Rule 15(1)

    The notice for the shareholder resolution must disclose, at minimum:

  • Name of the related party and nature of the relationship.
  • Nature, duration and material terms of the contract or arrangement.
  • Any advance paid or received for the contract or arrangement.
  • The manner of determining the pricing and other commercial terms, indicating whether it was arrived at through an independent price determination process.
  • Any other information relevant or important for members to take a decision.

    • Regulation 23 — the listed-company overlay

    Applies in addition to, not instead of, Section 188

    Materiality — Schedule XII (SEBI LODR Fifth Amendment, 2025)

    The earlier flat "₹1,000 crore or 10% of consolidated turnover, whichever is lower" test was replaced with a graded, turnover-linked scale, effective from the Fifth Amendment Regulations notified 18 November 2025:

    Turnover up to ₹20,000 crore10% of consolidated turnover
    ₹20,000 – ₹40,000 crore₹2,000 cr + 5% of turnover above ₹20,000 cr
    Above ₹40,000 crore₹3,000 cr + 2.5% of turnover above ₹40,000 cr (capped at ₹5,000 cr)

    Brand or royalty transactions use a separate, tighter threshold — 2% of annual consolidated turnover — instead of the graded scale. SME-listed entities use a flat ₹50 crore or 10% of turnover, whichever is lower.

    Subsidiary-level transactions

    Where a related-party transaction is entered into by a subsidiary and the listed entity itself is not a party, the listed entity's Audit Committee must still approve it in advance if the value exceeds the lower of:

  • 10% of the subsidiary's annual standalone turnover (per its last audited financials); or
  • The listed entity's own Schedule XII materiality threshold.

    • If the subsidiary has less than a year of audited standalone financials, the comparator becomes 10% of its aggregate paid-up capital and securities premium (measured within 3 months of seeking approval), against the same Schedule XII threshold.

    Omnibus approval validity

    Granted atValid until
    Annual General MeetingThe next AGM, within the timelines prescribed under Section 96 of the Companies Act
    Any other general meeting (incl. postal ballot)A maximum of one year from the date of approval

    Disclosure & other requirements

  • RPT disclosures to the stock exchange within 15 days of publishing financial results, on a half-yearly basis.
  • A Board-approved RPT policy on materiality and dealing with related parties, published on the company website and reviewed at least once every 3 years.
  • High Value Debt Listed Entities (HVDLEs): from 1 April 2025, material RPTs additionally require a No-Objection Certificate from the Debenture Trustee, who in turn seeks one from unrelated debenture holders.

    • SEBI has amended Regulation 23 repeatedly over 2023–2026 — always confirm the current Schedule XII figures and disclosure formats against the latest SEBI Master Circular before relying on them for a filing.

    Consequences of non-compliance

    Section 188(3)–(5)

    Voidable contract

    A contract entered into without the Board or shareholder consent required under Section 188 is voidable at the option of the Board. If not ratified by the Board or, where required, by shareholders within 3 months of the date the contract was entered into, any director or employee responsible must indemnify the company against any resulting loss.

    Penalty — Section 188(5)

    Company typeConsequence for officer in default
    Listed companyImprisonment up to 1 year, or fine, or both, as prescribed under the section
    Any other companyFine as prescribed under the section

    Exact fine quantum has been revised more than once, including through decriminalisation reforms under the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2020 — confirm the current figures under Section 188(5) before advising on a specific case.

    SEBI LODR consequences

  • Monetary penalties and directions under the SEBI Act for non-compliance with Regulation 23, including disclosure lapses.
  • Potential action against the listed entity, its directors and KMPs where an RPT is used to divert value from the company or its minority shareholders.
  • Reputational and governance-rating consequences, including qualified audit opinions where RPT approvals were not obtained as required.
    • If an RPT is structured to defraud the company, shareholders, or creditors, it may separately attract action under Section 447 (fraud) of the Companies Act, independent of the Section 188 consequences above.
