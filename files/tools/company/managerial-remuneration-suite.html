Appointment of managerial personnel — Section 196

Managing Director, Whole-Time Director, or Manager

Eligibility conditions

  • Age between 21 and 70 years — below 21 or above 70 requires a special resolution, with the explanatory statement justifying the appointment (Schedule V, Part I).
  • Not an undischarged insolvent, and has not at any time been adjudged insolvent.
  • Has not at any time suspended payment to creditors, or made a composition with them.
  • Has not at any time been convicted of an offence and sentenced to imprisonment for more than 6 months.
  • Not sentenced to imprisonment for 5 years or more under specified enactments (Schedule V, Part I) in the preceding 5 years.
  • Not detained under the Conservation of Foreign Exchange and Prevention of Smuggling Activities Act (COFEPOSA), 1974.

    • Tenure

    Appointment cannot exceed 5 years at a time. Re-appointment cannot be made earlier than 1 year before the expiry of the current term.

    Approval process

    Board
    • Approval at a duly convened Board meeting — not by circular resolution.
    • Terms of appointment and remuneration approved together.
    Shareholders
    • Ordinary resolution at the next general meeting is sufficient where the Schedule V, Part I conditions are met.
    • Special resolution required where those conditions are not met (e.g. age outside 21–70).
    The requirement of Central Government approval for appointments outside Schedule V conditions was removed by the 2018 amendment — a special resolution now suffices instead. Section 196(4) itself was relaxed from special to ordinary resolution by the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2020, except where Part I conditions aren't met.

    Return of appointment

    A return of appointment in Form MR-1 must be filed with the Registrar within 60 days of the appointment of an MD, WTD or Manager.

    Section 197 — the full picture

    Applies to public companies; private companies are outside the percentage caps

    CapLimit
    Overall managerial remuneration11% of net profit (computed u/s 198), for all directors and manager together
    Single MD / WTD / Manager5% of net profit
    More than one such person10% of net profit in aggregate
    Non-executive directors, if an MD/WTD/Manager exists1% of net profit
    Non-executive directors, if none exists3% of net profit

    "Net profit" here is the Section 198 figure — a specific computation starting from accounting profit, adding back items such as the managerial remuneration itself, and excluding certain capital profits — not the P&L figure or EBITDA.

    Sitting fees, commission & independent directors

  • Sitting fees for attending Board or committee meetings — capped at ₹1,00,000 per meeting under Rule 4 of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules, 2014.
  • Non-executive and independent directors may receive a profit-related commission, subject to Board and shareholder approval within the applicable cap.
  • Independent directors are not entitled to stock options under any circumstance — only fees, reimbursement, and profit-related commission.

    • Insurance premium — Section 197(13)

    Premium paid by the company on a policy indemnifying any of its directors, MD, WTD, manager, or KMP against liability in respect of any negligence, default, misfeasance, breach of duty or breach of trust is not treated as remuneration — unless that person is proved to be guilty, in which case the premium paid on their behalf is treated as remuneration.

    Private companies Section 197's percentage ceilings do not apply to private companies at all. Other governance and disclosure requirements may still apply depending on the company's other characteristics (e.g. Section 203 KMP rules, Section 204 secretarial audit).

    Recovery of remuneration — Section 199

    The clawback provision

    Where a company is required to restate its financial statements due to fraud or non-compliance with any requirement of the Act or the rules, and it is found that the MD, WTD, Manager or CEO received remuneration in excess of what would have been payable based on the restated figures, the company must recover that excess from them — for a period covering the two preceding financial years.

    Recovery under Section 199 does not require proof of personal wrongdoing by the individual — it is triggered by the fact of restatement due to fraud or non-compliance, and the resulting overpayment.

    Compensation for loss of office — Section 202

    Restrictions on payment to an MD, WTD or Manager on ceasing to hold office

    The cap

    Any payment for loss of office cannot exceed the remuneration the person would have earned for the remainder of their term, or 3 years, whichever is shorter — computed on the basis of the average remuneration actually drawn during the preceding 3 years (or the actual period served, if shorter).

    When no such payment may be made

  • Where the company is being wound up due to the director's own negligence or default.
  • Where the director resigns following an actual or expected reconstruction or amalgamation, and is offered re-employment on similar or better terms in the reconstructed/amalgamated company.
  • Where the director is guilty of fraud, breach of trust, gross negligence or mismanagement in relation to the company's affairs.
  • Where the director resigns otherwise than in connection with a reconstruction or amalgamation, and was in a position to receive payment in circumstances not covered by the permitted exceptions.

    • Section 203 — mandatory KMP

    Applicability: every listed company, and every public company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more

    Who must be appointed

    i.MD, CEO or Manager

    In their absence, a Whole-Time Director.

    ii.Company Secretary

    A whole-time CS, qualified under the Company Secretaries Act.

    iii.Chief Financial Officer

    Responsible for the financial affairs of the company.

    Chairperson / MD-CEO separation

    One individual cannot ordinarily hold the office of Chairperson and MD or CEO of the same company at the same time — unless the company's articles provide otherwise, or the company does not carry on multiple businesses. Certain classes of listed companies carry a further exemption under SEBI LODR.

    Other constraints

  • A vacancy in any whole-time KMP office must be filled by the Board within 6 months.
  • An individual cannot be KMP of more than one company at the same time — except as a director in another company, or as permitted for KMP of a holding company also being a director of its subsidiary.

    • Section 205 — functions of the Company Secretary

  • Report to the Board on compliance with the Act, rules and other applicable laws.
  • Ensure the company complies with applicable secretarial standards.
  • Discharge such other duties as prescribed under the rules.

    • Section 204 — Secretarial Audit

    Independent verification of secretarial and legal compliance

    Applicability

  • Every listed company.
  • Every public company with paid-up share capital of ₹50 crore or more.
  • Every public company with turnover of ₹250 crore or more.
  • Every company with outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions of ₹100 crore or more (added by the 2020 amendment to the Rules).

    • The report

    A Practising Company Secretary conducts the audit and issues the report in Form MR-3, which is annexed to the Board's Report. The Board must explain, in its report, any qualification or observation made by the Secretarial Auditor.

    Secretarial audit is distinct from statutory financial audit — it verifies compliance with company law, SEBI regulations, FEMA and other applicable corporate laws, not the accuracy of financial statements.

    Consequences of non-compliance

    Section 197 & related provisions

    Excess remuneration

    Any remuneration drawn in excess of what Section 197 or Schedule V permits must be refunded to the company by the recipient. The company cannot waive recovery of this excess without the approval required under the Act (a special resolution, where applicable) — a director cannot simply be let off by Board decision alone.

    Holding office in contravention

    If a managerial person or director receives remuneration in contravention of Section 197, they hold the excess amount in trust for the company until repayment. Continued retention without repayment compounds the contravention.

    General penalty — Section 172

    Where no specific penalty is prescribed for a particular contravention under this Chapter, the company and every officer in default are liable under the general penalty provision, Section 172 of the Act.

    Where excess remuneration or improper compensation for loss of office is used to divert value from the company or its shareholders, this may separately attract action for oppression and mismanagement, or under Section 447 (fraud), independent of the Chapter XIII consequences above.

    Clawback non-recovery

    Failure to recover excess remuneration required under Section 199 following a restatement is itself a contravention — the burden falls on the company and its officers to pursue recovery once the restatement establishes the overpayment.
