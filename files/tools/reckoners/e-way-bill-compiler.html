What the E-Way Bill is

Who's responsible, and how Form EWB-01 is structured

An E-Way Bill (EWB) is an electronic document that must exist before a vehicle carrying goods above a value threshold begins moving. It's generated on the government e-Way Bill portal under Section 68 of the CGST Act and Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, 2017. On generation, the system issues a unique 12-digit E-Way Bill Number (EBN) — the reference used by supplier, recipient, and transporter alike.

Form EWB-01 · Part A Consignment
  • Parties — GSTIN of supplier and recipient
  • Route — place of dispatch and delivery (PIN codes)
  • Document — invoice/challan number, date, HSN code, value
  • Purpose — reason for transport: supply, return, job work, etc.

Filed by whoever causes the movement — typically the supplier or recipient — before goods leave.

Form EWB-01 · Part B Transport
  • Vehicle — registration number for road transport
  • Transporter ID — for rail, air, or ship movement
  • Mode — road / rail / air / ship

Filed by the transporter (or self, if self-transporting). The validity clock starts only once this is complete.

Who's responsible

  • Registered consignor/consignee — whoever causes the movement generates the EWB.
  • Unregistered supplier → registered recipient — the recipient is responsible, as if they were the supplier.
  • Transporter — generates it if neither party has, once goods are handed over.

    • Generation channels: web portal (ewaybillgst.gov.in), SMS, Android app, API/site-to-site integration, and GSPs. Turnover above ₹5 crore requires 6-digit HSN codes; below that, 4-digit codes are acceptable.

    Trigger conditions beyond the plain value test

  • Any supply crossing the threshold — sale, transfer, or barter above the applicable limit.
  • Inward supply from an unregistered person — registered recipient is responsible for generation.
  • Inter-state job work movement — required even if value is under ₹50,000.
  • Stock transfers between branches — applies once value crosses the applicable threshold, including across states.
  • Sales returns / goods on approval — above the relevant threshold.
  • Exhibition / fair consignments — treated as a taxable movement above threshold.
  • Principal ↔ job-worker movement — for goods sent for further processing.
  • E-commerce consignments — dispatched by/through an operator, above threshold.

    • Exemptions

    Rule 138(14) exempts roughly 150 goods and several transport scenarios, irrespective of value

    By nature of goods

    Fresh fruit, vegetables, milk, unprocessed meat and fish, eggs, live animals, currency, used personal/household effects, LPG for domestic supply, kerosene under the PDS, postal baggage, and coral.

    Precious goods (Chapter 71)

    Pearls, precious/semi-precious stones, precious metals, and jewellery — except imitation jewellery. Several states still enable a state-specific EWB for intra-state gold movement.

    Non-GST / specified fuels

    Alcoholic liquor for human consumption, petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, petrol, natural gas, and aviation turbine fuel.

    By mode of transport

    Non-motorised conveyances (handcarts, cycles, cycle-rickshaws) regardless of value, and empty cargo containers being repositioned.

    Short / supervised / in-transit movement

    Weighbridge round trips within 20 km with a delivery challan on return; movement under customs supervision or bond between port/airport/ICD/CFS/land customs station; transit cargo to Nepal or Bhutan.

    Government & defence movement

    Rail movement where Central/State Government or a local authority is consignor, and notified defence-related consignments.

    Value-independent: exemption for listed goods depends on what's moving, not its value — ₹10 lakh of fresh vegetables still needs no EWB. States can still narrow this for intra-state movement of sensitive goods.

    One extension window: 8 hours before to 8 hours after expiry. Total validity (original + extensions) is capped at 360 days from original generation.

    Counting rule: regular cargo gets 1 day per 200 km or part thereof (500 km = 3 days). ODC/multimodal-with-ship gets 1 day per 20 km. Each day runs until midnight of its due date. Delay from natural calamity, law-and-order issues, or transhipment can justify an extension beyond distance-based validity — assessed by the officer, not this calculator.

    Extend, cancel, reject

    The three post-generation actions, their windows, and the generation steps

    Extend Time-bound

    Requestable 8 hours before to 8 hours after expiry. Update current location and delay reason. Total validity capped at 360 days from original generation.

    Cancel 24 hrs

    Within 24 hours of generation, if goods weren't transported or details were wrong. Not possible once verified in transit.

    Reject 72 hrs

    Named recipient can reject an EWB that isn't theirs, within 72 hours of generation or before delivery — whichever is earlier.

    Generation, step by step

    Log in to the portal

    ewaybillgst.gov.in with GSTIN credentials and mandatory two-factor authentication.

    Select "Generate New"

    Choose sub-type: outward supply, inward supply, job work, SKD/CKD, recipient not known, etc.

    Fill Part A

    GSTINs, dispatch/delivery place, invoice/challan number and date, HSN code, taxable value.

    Fill Part B

    Vehicle number or transporter ID and transport document number — starts the validity clock.

    Generate and carry the EBN

    System issues the 12-digit E-Way Bill Number and printable/QR-coded EWB-01.

    Penalties

    Risk exposure under Sections 129, 122, and 130 of the CGST Act

    Minor errors (vehicle number typo, pin code slip)₹1,000

    Circular 64/38/2018-GST provides a flat reduced penalty for small clerical mistakes, when the rest of the documentation is in order.

    No/expired EWB on taxable goods, owner comes forward200% of tax

    Release conditional on tax plus a penalty equal to the tax amount, under Section 129 as amended by the Finance Act, 2021.

    Exempt goods, no documentation2% of value or ₹25,000

    Whichever is lower — reflecting that no tax revenue was at stake.

    General contravention (Section 122)₹10,000 or tax evaded

    Whichever is higher — a backstop that applies even outside a roadside interception.

    Process: an officer can detain goods and conveyance on the spot; a detention notice follows within 24 hours; the person in charge has 7 days to respond and pay before escalation to confiscation under Section 130. Courts have distinguished genuine evasion from harmless procedural slips (e.g. an EWB that expired due to traffic delay) — but that's argued case by case. Treat these figures as the framework, not a litigation guarantee — they're periodically revisited by the GST Council.

    2025–26 updates

    What changed in the current compliance cycle

    From 1 Jan 2025
    180-day document age cap

    No EWB can be generated against an invoice/challan older than 180 days from its own date.

    From 1 Jan 2025
    360-day validity ceiling

    Extensions can't push total validity beyond 360 days from the original generation date.

    Ongoing
    Mandatory multi-factor authentication

    2FA/MFA compulsory for all taxpayers and transporters on the e-Way Bill and e-Invoice systems.

    From 1 Aug 2026
    Ship-to GSTIN & voluntary closure

    Ship-to GSTIN becomes mandatory in IRN & EWB APIs when ship-to details exist ("URP" if unregistered). B2B/SEZ ship-to details from the e-Invoice stage can't be silently overridden at EWB creation. A new voluntary closure facility lets suppliers, recipients, or transporters mark delivery complete.
