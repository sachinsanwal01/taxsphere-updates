Overview & concept

What changes — and what doesn't — under the Income-tax Act, 2025

A Non-Profit Organisation (NPO) in India is any trust, society, Section 8 company, fund, university, hospital or similar entity constituted for a charitable or religious purpose rather than for profit. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), every such entity that holds a valid registration is uniformly termed a Registered Non-Profit Organisation (RNPO) — replacing the old collection of labels ("charitable trust", "charitable institution", "fund", "institution under Section 10(23C)") that existed across the Income-tax Act, 1961.

The 2025 Act does not change the underlying tax policy for NPOs. It consolidates roughly a dozen scattered sections of the 1961 Act — 2(15), 10(23C), 11, 12, 12A, 12AA, 12AB, 13, 80G, 115BBC, 115TD — into one self-contained code: Part B of Chapter XVII (Sections 332–355), cutting the word count from ~12,800 to ~7,600 and replacing "previous year / assessment year" with a single unified "tax year."

Charitable purpose — Section 2(23)

Unchanged in substance from Section 2(15) of the 1961 Act

2(23)(a)
Relief of the poor

Includes relief to the economically and socially disadvantaged.

2(23)(b)
Education

Formal and non-formal education, including skill development.

2(23)(c)
Yoga

Retained as a standalone charitable head (added in 2015).

2(23)(d)
Medical relief

Includes hospitals, dispensaries and public health activity.

2(23)(e)
Preservation of environment

Watersheds, forests, wildlife and pollution control.

2(23)(f)
Preservation of monuments

Monuments or places/objects of artistic or historic interest.

2(23)(g)
Any other object of general public utility (GPU)

The residual, most-litigated head — subject to the strictest commercial-activity limits (see Sec 346).

Core vocabulary of the RNPO framework

RNPO
Sec 355(g)

Any person with a valid registration under Section 332, or under a specified provision of the 1961 Act (12A/12AA/12AB/10(23C)) that has not expired or been cancelled.

Regular income
Sec 335

Activity income, property/investment income, voluntary contributions, and business-undertaking income — the base for the 85% application test.

Specified income
Sec 337

Anonymous donations beyond the threshold, related-party benefit, foreign application, non-prescribed investment, and similar misuse — taxed at a flat 30%.

Residual income
—

Income that is neither regular nor specified; taxed at the RNPO's normal applicable rate.

Corpus donation
Sec 339

A voluntary contribution given with the donor's specific written direction that it form part of corpus — excluded from the 85% test if invested per Sec 350.

Application of income
Sec 341

Spending regular income on the RNPO's charitable/religious objects in India; only 85% of a donation to another RNPO counts as application.

Accumulation
Sec 342–343

Formal set-aside for up to 5 years (Form 109) or an automatic 15% deemed accumulation, both invested per Sec 350.

Tax year
—

The single unified concept (1 April–31 March) that now replaces both "previous year" and "assessment year."

This tool is a plain-language guide, not the statute. Always cross-check the applicable Section against the Income-tax Act, 2025 and the Income-tax Rules, 2026 before acting.

Registration — Section 332

Replaces Sections 12A, 12AA, 12AB and 10(23C) of the 1961 Act

Eligibility conditions

  • Condition (a): Constituted, registered or incorporated in India for one or more charitable purposes under Section 2(23), for public religious purposes, or both.
  • Condition (b): Properties held for the benefit of the general public under an irrevocable trust, wholly for charitable or religious purposes in India (entities constituted before the 1961 Act may qualify partly). The explicit "irrevocable trust" requirement is new in statute — earlier it existed only through judicial interpretation.

    • Section 355(p) clarifies that "wholly for charitable or religious purposes" covers wholly charitable, wholly religious, or wholly both — resolving earlier ambiguity for mixed-purpose trusts.

    The seven registration situations — Section 332(3)

    Sl. 1 — Activities not yet commenced Form 104

    Provisional registration, granted within 1 month of application; valid 3 years.

    Sl. 2 — Provisional registration about to expire Form 105

    Apply at least 6 months before expiry, or within 6 months of commencing activities — whichever is earlier.

    Sl. 3–7 — Regular registration / renewal / modification of objects Form 105

    Covers first regular registration after activities commence, renewal of a 5- or 10-year registration, and re-registration after a change of objects.

    Validity periods

    SituationValidity
    Provisional registration (Sl. 1)3 tax years
    Regular registration — standard5 tax years
    Regular registration — total income ≤ ₹5 crore in each of preceding 2 tax years (Sec 332(5))10 tax years

    Transition for existing NGOs

    No fresh application needed on 1 April 2026 Any entity holding a valid, uncancelled registration under Section 12A, 12AA, 12AB or 10(23C) of the 1961 Act automatically transitions to RNPO status. Tax benefits continue seamlessly until the existing registration's own expiry date, at which point renewal follows the Section 332 timelines above.
    Late applications (Sl. 3C/4C/5C/7C) can be condoned by the Principal Commissioner/Commissioner for "reasonable cause" — inadvertence alone is usually not accepted. If the delay is not condoned, the RNPO becomes liable to exit tax on accreted income under Section 352.

    Classification of income — Sections 334–339

    Every rupee an RNPO receives falls into one of three buckets

    Sec 335
    Regular

    Activity receipts, property/investment income, voluntary contributions, business-undertaking and incidental-business income. Exempt (Sec 336) if 85% is applied/accumulated per Sections 341–342.

    Sec 337 · @30%
    Specified

    Anonymous donations beyond the threshold, related-party benefit, income applied outside India without CBDT permission, non-prescribed investment, inter-charity donation of accumulated income, accumulated income unused within timelines, AO-determined excess income.

    Residual
    Balance

    Income that is neither regular nor specified — taxed at the RNPO's ordinary applicable rate, same as any other assessee.

    Tax rates — Section 334

    Income typeRate
    Specified income (Sec 337)Flat 30%
    Taxable regular income (shortfall against the 85% rule, if not accumulated)Rate applicable to the RNPO
    Anonymous donations — Section 337(1) Not taxable up to the higher of ₹1,00,000 or 5% of total donations received in the tax year; the excess is taxed at 30%. RNPOs established wholly for religious purposes are excluded from this tax entirely.

    Corpus & deemed corpus donations

    Sec 339
    Corpus donation

    A voluntary contribution given with the donor's specific written direction that it form part of the RNPO's corpus. Excluded from regular income and from the 85% test — provided it is invested/deposited in a mode permitted under Section 350.

    Sec 340
    Deemed corpus donation

    Certain contributions are treated as corpus by deeming fiction, subject to conditions specified in the section — same investment discipline under Section 350 applies.

    Income applied outside India is normally taxed at 30% as specified income (Sec 338), unless the CBDT permits it by general or special order — available only to RNPOs established before 1 April 1952 for charitable or religious purposes, in narrow circumstances.

    Application & accumulation of income — Sections 341–343

    How an RNPO satisfies the 85% application requirement

    1. Direct application — Section 341

  • At least 85% of regular income must be applied for charitable/religious purposes in India during the tax year.
  • A donation to another RNPO counts as application only to the extent of 85% of the amount donated.
  • Amounts applied out of corpus or out of a loan are not treated as application — but reinvesting the corpus, or repaying the loan, within 5 years is allowed as application in that later year.
  • A shortfall can be treated as deemed application under Section 341(5)/(7) by exercising an option in Form 108, before the return due date under Section 263(1), subject to conditions on when the amount must actually be spent.

    • 2. Formal accumulation — Section 342

    How it works
    • Income may be accumulated/set apart for a specific purpose for up to 5 years (court-order/injunction periods excluded).
    • A statement of accumulation must be filed in Form 109 on or before the return due date under Section 263(1).
    • Accumulated funds must be invested only in modes permitted under Section 350.
    Repurposing & dissolution
    • Change of purpose is allowed with prior Assessing Officer approval, via Form 110.
    • Paying accumulated income to another RNPO is not treated as application — except on dissolution, where the AO may allow transfer to a similarly-placed RNPO.

    3. Deemed (statutory) accumulation — Section 343

    Regular income, less amounts actually applied and formally accumulated, is automatically treated as deemed accumulated income up to 15% of regular income — and must likewise be invested in modes permitted under Section 350. This is the mechanical equivalent of the old "15% set-apart" that most RNPOs use by default even without filing anything extra.

    Failing the 85% test — without either applying, formally accumulating (Form 109), or exercising the Form 108 option — turns the shortfall into taxable regular income for that year.

    Commercial activities — Sections 344–346

    RNPOs are not businesses — the Act keeps a tight leash on trading activity

    Business undertaking as property
    Sec 344

    An RNPO may hold a business undertaking as part of its property (equivalent to the old Section 11(4)); the Assessing Officer can determine income in excess of what the books show.

    General restriction
    Sec 345

    An RNPO cannot otherwise carry on commercial activity unless it is incidental to attaining its objects, and separate books of account are maintained for it.

    GPU-specific cap
    Sec 346

    An RNPO advancing "any other object of general public utility" may run commercial activity incidental to that object only if receipts from it do not exceed 20% of total receipts, with separate books maintained.

    Carrying on non-incidental commercial activity — or breaching the 20% GPU cap — can jeopardise RNPO status entirely and is treated as a specified violation under Section 351.

    Compliances — Sections 347–350

    Books, audit, return of income, and permitted investments

    Books of account — Section 347

  • Cash book, ledger, journal, bills, receipts and vouchers sufficient to present a true and fair view of the RNPO's affairs.
  • Separate books for any business undertaking (Sec 344) and for GPU-incidental commercial activity (Sec 346).
  • Project-wise records distinguishing charitable income, voluntary contributions, commercial income, specified income and residual income.
  • Records of application (India and abroad, with payee and purpose), accumulation (Sec 342 — year, purpose, amount applied, balance), investments under Section 350, corpus donations, and loans/borrowings/repayments.

    • Audit — Section 348

    Where the RNPO's income exceeds the basic exemption threshold before claiming the Chapter's exemptions, its accounts must be audited by a Chartered Accountant. The report is filed in Form 112 — a single consolidated form replacing the earlier Form 10B and Form 10BB.

    Small vs Large Registered NPO — Form 112 classification

    Determines how much of Form 112 an auditee must complete

    Form 112 itself classifies every auditee as either a Small Registered NPO or a Large Registered NPO. An RNPO is a Large Registered NPO if any one of the following is satisfied for the tax year; otherwise it is a Small Registered NPO:

    ConditionThreshold
    (a) Regular income (Sec 335)Exceeds ₹5 crore
    (b) Foreign contribution received in the tax yearExceeds ₹10 lakh
    (c) Application of income outside IndiaExceeds ₹10 lakh
    "Foreign contribution" here takes the same meaning as under Section 2(1)(h) of the FCRA, 2010. The classification is self-tested by the auditor on Form 112 itself (Row 6: if (a), (b) or (c) is "Yes", the auditee is large; otherwise small) and determines which additional schedules — related-party transactions, Section 337 specified-income details, Section 346 GPU commercial-activity compliance, and related disclosures — the auditor must complete.

    Return of income — Section 349

    The RNPO must file its return of income by the due date prescribed under Section 263(1) — typically the audit-linked due date where Section 348 audit applies.

    Permitted modes of investment — Section 350

    Broadly carried over from the old Section 11(5) list

  • Government securities and Post Office savings/deposit schemes.
  • Deposits with scheduled banks or co-operative banks (including term deposits).
  • Units of UTI and specified mutual funds; shares of specified public-sector or statutory companies.
  • Immovable property, and deposits with IFSC-based entities and other modes as prescribed by rule.

    • Corpus, accumulated (Sec 342) and deemed-accumulated (Sec 343) funds must all sit in these permitted modes; investing them elsewhere converts the amount into specified income taxable at 30%.

    Donations & donor-deduction approval — Section 354

    The 80G-equivalent route — now a separate application from RNPO registration

    Under the 2025 Act, becoming an RNPO (Section 332) and being able to issue donors a tax-deductible receipt (Section 354, referencing Section 133(1)(b)(ii)) are two distinct approvals. Most entities — public trusts, societies, Section 8 companies — must first hold Section 332 registration before applying for Section 354 approval, generally via the same Form 105. Entities listed in Schedule VII, Table Sl. No. 1 (certain government-linked funds and institutions) may apply for Section 354 approval directly, without prior Section 332 registration.

    Donations made before a cancellation date remain eligible for the donor's deduction; donations made after cancellation are not. Donors should be notified promptly on any cancellation.

    Annual donor-reporting forms

    113
    Statement of donations received was 10BD

    Annual donor-wise statement the RNPO files with the tax department.

    114
    Certificate of donation was 10BE

    Issued to each donor so they can claim the Section 133(1)(b)(ii) deduction in their own return.

    Corpus vs ordinary donation

    TypeTreatment
    Ordinary donationForms part of regular income; subject to the 85% application/accumulation test (Sec 341–343).
    Corpus donation (Sec 339)Excluded from regular income if invested per Sec 350 — not subject to the 85% test.
    Anonymous donation (Sec 337)Exempt up to the higher of ₹1,00,000 or 5% of total donations; excess taxed at 30% (wholly-religious RNPOs excluded).

    Violations & exit tax — Sections 351–353

    What can cost an RNPO its registration — and the one-time tax that follows

    Specified violation — Section 351

  • Income applied for a purpose other than the registered charitable/religious object.
  • Income or property applied, directly or indirectly, for the benefit of a related/specified person.
  • Activities found to be non-genuine, or not carried out in accordance with registration conditions.
  • Non-compliance with a requirement of any other law material to achieving the RNPO's objects.
  • Failure to file the return of income (Sec 349) as required.

    • Tax on accreted income — Section 352

    On cancellation of registration, conversion into a non-RNPO form, merger with an entity that is not a similarly-placed RNPO, or dissolution without transferring assets to another RNPO with similar objects, the RNPO is liable to a one-time "exit tax" computed on its accreted income — broadly, the fair market value of its assets less its liabilities on the specified date. This tax is levied at the maximum marginal rate and can be far larger than years of the exemption it once claimed, so it is treated as a last-resort consequence rather than routine tax planning.

    Other violations — Section 353

    Covers consequential matters such as denial of exemption for the relevant tax year and other administrative consequences of non-compliance, distinct from the accreted-income exit tax under Section 352.

    Condonation of a late Form 104/105 application is available from the Principal Commissioner/Commissioner only for demonstrated "reasonable cause" — plain inadvertence is generally not accepted, and an uncondoned lapse can itself trigger Section 352 exit tax.

    FCRA compliance

    Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 — administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs, independent of the Income-tax Act

    Any NPO that wishes to accept a contribution from a foreign source must either hold FCRA registration or obtain prior permission for a specific one-off contribution. This is a separate compliance track from Income-tax RNPO registration — an entity can be a fully compliant RNPO and still be barred from receiving foreign funds without FCRA clearance.

    Key requirements

  • Ordinarily available to an entity that has existed for at least 3 years and has undertaken reasonable charitable activity/spend in that period (or, for newer entities, via prior permission for a specific donor and purpose).
  • Foreign contribution must be received only in the designated FCRA account at the SBI, New Delhi Main Branch, with a separate utilisation account permitted at any scheduled bank.
  • Administrative expenses are capped at 20% of the foreign contribution utilised in a financial year.
  • Registration is valid for 5 years; renewal (Form FC-3C) must be filed before expiry.
  • Annual return in Form FC-4 is mandatory, whether or not any foreign contribution was received in the year.

    • FCRA registration/renewal and Income-tax RNPO registration/renewal run on independent clocks — track both separately in your compliance calendar.

    GST for NPOs

    Registered NPO status does not mean automatic GST exemption

    An NPO is subject to GST registration on the same turnover thresholds as any other entity (₹20 lakh for services, ₹40 lakh for goods, lower in special category states) once it makes a "supply" for consideration. RNPO status only triggers a narrow, specific exemption — it does not exempt every activity an NPO undertakes.

    What is exempt

  • Services by an entity registered as charitable (formerly under Sec 12AA/12AB, now an RNPO) by way of specified charitable activities — relief of the poor, afflicted, disabled, orphans or the abandoned; advancement of religion, spirituality or yoga; educational programmes for the abandoned, orphans, prisoners or the physically/mentally challenged; and environment preservation.
  • Certain renting of religious precincts below prescribed tariff/rent thresholds, and specified education services by recognised institutions.

    • What is typically taxable

  • General Public Utility (GPU) activities that don't fall within the narrower GST definition of "charitable activities" — e.g. fee-based skill training outside the exempt categories, consultancy, or event services.
  • Renting of commercial space, sale of goods/merchandise, and sponsorship income.
  • Membership/subscription fees — an area of ongoing litigation following judicial rulings on the doctrine of mutuality.
    • The GST definition of "charitable activities" (Notification 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended) is narrower than the Income-tax Act's Section 2(23) definition — an activity can be a valid charitable purpose for income-tax exemption while still attracting GST.

    Step-by-step registration process

    From incorporation to a fully compliant RNPO

    Choose a legal structure & incorporate

    Trust (Trust Deed + Sub-Registrar), Society (Memorandum + Registrar of Societies), or Section 8 Company (MCA/ROC) — pick based on governance style and funder expectations.

    Draft objects aligned to Section 2(23)

    Ensure the founding document's objects clause maps clearly to a recognised charitable purpose (or public religious purpose).

    Obtain PAN & open a bank account

    Required before any registration application can be filed.

    File Form 104 for provisional registration

    Section 332(3) Sl. No. 1, where activities have not yet commenced — typically granted within 1 month.

    Commence activities within the 3-year provisional window

    Maintain books from day one (Sec 347), even before regular registration is granted.

    Apply for regular registration on Form 105

    At least 6 months before provisional expiry, or within 6 months of commencing activities — whichever is earlier.

    Apply for Section 354 approval (donor deduction)

    A separate application (usually the same Form 105) so donors can claim a deduction on their contributions.

    Register under FCRA if foreign funding is expected

    Ordinarily only after ~3 years of demonstrated charitable activity.

    Register for GST if taxable supplies cross the threshold

    Charitable-activity exemption is narrow — review each revenue stream separately.

    Run the annual cycle

    Books (Sec 347) → Audit/Form 112 (Sec 348) → Return of income (Sec 349) → Form 109/108 as needed → Form 113/114 for donors.
