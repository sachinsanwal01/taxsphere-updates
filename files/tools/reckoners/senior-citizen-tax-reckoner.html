Overview

Who counts as a senior citizen, and what changes for them

Indian income tax gives resident individuals extra relief purely on account of age. None of this changes the tax base or the definition of income — it simply raises certain thresholds, adds certain deductions, and removes certain compliance burdens once a person crosses 60 (and again at 75 and 80). These benefits sit across the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Chapter VIII deductions, advance tax, TDS) and are almost entirely restricted to residents — a non-resident senior citizen gets none of the age-based reliefs below except where a section says otherwise.

Age 60–79
Senior Citizen

Resident individual who is 60 years or older at any time during the tax year.

Age 80+
Super Senior Citizen

Resident individual who is 80 years or older at any time during the tax year — an additional, higher tier of benefits.

Age 75+
"Specified" senior citizen

The narrower category used only for the Section 194P return-filing exemption (pension + same-bank interest only).

Residency
Must be resident

Age-based basic-exemption slabs, advance tax relief, and the higher TDS threshold are all conditioned on being a resident in the tax year.

Advance tax exemption — Section 404 (erstwhile Section 207)

Who is excused from paying advance tax instalments

Ordinarily, under Section 404 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (carrying forward old Section 208), any person whose estimated tax liability for the tax year is ₹10,000 or more must pay it in advance, in quarterly instalments, rather than in one lump sum at return-filing time.

The senior-citizen carve-out A resident senior citizen (60 years or older at any time in the tax year) who has no income from business or profession is not liable to pay advance tax at all — regardless of how large the tax liability is. The entire amount can instead be paid as self-assessment tax when the return is filed, with no interest under Sections 234B/234C-equivalent provisions for "non-payment" of advance tax.

Conditions — both must be satisfied

  • Resident in India for the tax year (a non-resident senior citizen does not qualify).
  • Aged 60 or above at any time during the tax year (not necessarily on 1 April).
  • No income chargeable under "Profits and gains of business or profession." Rental income, pension, interest, capital gains and salary do not disqualify the exemption — a proprietorship, freelance consultancy, or trading activity does.

    • Worked examples

    Exempt
    • Resident, aged 68, earns ₹40,000/month in rent and has a fixed-deposit interest income — no business income → fully exempt from advance tax, whatever the tax liability.
    Not exempt
    • Resident, aged 61, runs a small proprietorship business alongside retirement income → must pay advance tax in full, like any other taxpayer, because of the business income.
    • Resident, aged 59, large interest income → must pay, since the age condition (60+) isn't met.
    This is relief from the instalment schedule only — it does not reduce the actual tax payable. All of it is still due, just at self-assessment stage rather than in four advance instalments through the year.

    Age-based enhancement exists only under the old regime

    Senior citizen (60–79)
    ₹3L

    Higher old-regime basic exemption — resident only.

    Super senior citizen (80+)
    ₹5L

    Highest old-regime basic exemption tier.

    New (default) regime — Section 202 — is age-blind Under the new regime, every individual — 25 or 85 — is taxed on the same slabs, with a flat basic exemption of ₹4,00,000. There is no higher slab-start for senior or super senior citizens here. What does help retirees under this regime is the Section 87A rebate — up to ₹60,000, effectively making income up to ₹12,00,000 (₹12,75,000 for salary/pension income, after the ₹75,000 standard deduction) tax-free for residents, regardless of age.

    Choosing between the two regimes is a case-by-case computation — the old regime's higher age-based exemption plus 80TTB/80D/80DDB deductions can beat the new regime for a senior citizen with large interest, medical or insurance outgo, while the new regime's rebate often wins for those with modest income and few deductions. Run both before filing.

    Interest-income deduction — Section 153 (erstwhile 80TTA/80TTB)

    Old regime only — not available if the new regime is chosen

    General taxpayer
    Erstwhile Sec 80TTA

    Deduction capped at ₹10,000, and only for interest on a savings account (bank, co-operative bank or post office) — fixed-deposit interest is not covered.

    Senior citizen (60+)
    Erstwhile Sec 80TTB

    Deduction capped at ₹50,000, covering interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits and recurring deposits with banks, co-operative banks or the post office. Replaces 80TTA entirely — a senior citizen cannot claim both.

    Both provisions now sit together under Section 153 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, in Chapter VIII. Some recent commentary flags a proposed increase of the senior-citizen cap to ₹1,00,000 — this had not consistently been confirmed across sources at the time this tool was built, so treat ₹50,000 as the settled figure and verify the current-year cap before filing.

    Section 153 (like the rest of Chapter VIII) is available only if the old tax regime is opted for. Under the new (default) regime, this deduction cannot be claimed at all — interest income is fully taxable there, offset only by the basic exemption and the Section 87A rebate.

    TDS threshold on interest income

    A deduction-at-source concession, not a tax exemption — all TDS provisions now sit under the consolidated Section 393

    PayeeTDS threshold per payer, per year
    General taxpayer₹50,000
    Resident senior citizen (60+)₹1,00,000
    This threshold only decides when the bank deducts TDS — it does not make the interest itself tax-free. Interest above the basic exemption and applicable deductions remains fully taxable; a senior citizen must still report all interest income in the return and pay any tax due, even if no TDS was deducted below the threshold.

    A resident senior citizen whose total income (after deductions) is expected to be nil can also submit Form 15H to each bank at the start of the tax year, so that no TDS is deducted at all during the year — this is different from, and additional to, the ₹1,00,000 threshold above.

    Specified-disease treatment — Section 128 (erstwhile 80DDB)

    CategoryDeduction cap
    Below 60₹40,000
    Senior citizen or super senior citizen (60+)₹1,00,000

    Available for expenditure actually incurred on the medical treatment of specified diseases and ailments (as prescribed by rule) for the taxpayer or a dependent — reduced by any amount received under insurance or from an employer.

    Return-filing relief — Section 194P mechanism

    For resident "specified" senior citizens aged 75 and above

    A resident individual aged 75 or above, whose only sources of income are a pension and interest income from the very same bank that pays the pension, can be relieved of the obligation to file an income-tax return at all — provided the conditions below are met.

    Conditions

  • Resident individual, aged 75 years or above during the tax year.
  • Income consists only of pension, and interest income from the same bank that credits the pension.
  • The bank is a "specified bank" notified for this purpose.
  • A declaration in the prescribed form is submitted to that bank.

    • Once the declaration is filed, the specified bank itself computes total income, applies the deductions available under Chapter VIII (e.g. Sections 126/128/153) and the Section 87A rebate, works out the final tax liability, and deducts it as TDS — at which point the senior citizen is not required to separately file a return of income for that year.

    This is a narrow administrative concession — a single rupee of any other income (rent, capital gains, dividend from another source, freelance income) takes the person out of this relief and back into the normal return-filing requirement.

    Other age-linked benefits

    Paper filing for super senior citizens
    Return filing

    A super senior citizen (80+) filing a simple return (equivalent to old ITR-1/ITR-4) may file in paper mode instead of mandatory e-filing.

    Form 15H
    TDS

    A resident senior citizen whose final tax liability for the year is expected to be nil can submit Form 15H to each payer of interest, stopping TDS deduction entirely for the year.

    Reverse mortgage receipts
    Exempt income

    Periodic or lump-sum amounts received by a senior citizen under a notified reverse mortgage scheme are not treated as taxable income — commonly relied on for equity release from a self-occupied house.

    Standard deduction on pension
    Not age-specific, but pension-relevant

    ₹75,000 under the new regime / ₹50,000 under the old regime against salary and (most) pension income — available to everyone, but especially relevant since pension is the dominant income source for most senior citizens.

    Section 87A rebate
    Age-blind, but retiree-relevant

    Up to ₹60,000 (new regime, income up to ₹12 lakh) or ₹12,500 (old regime, income up to ₹5 lakh) — available to all resident individuals regardless of age, often eliminating tax entirely for retirees with modest income.

    Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS)
    Not a tax exemption

    SCSS deposits qualify for the Section 123 (erstwhile 80C) deduction on investment, but the quarterly interest received remains fully taxable — a common point of confusion.
