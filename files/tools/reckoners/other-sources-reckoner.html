Income from Other Sources

The residuary head of income under Chapter IV of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — successor to Sec 56–65 of the 1961 Act. Covers what's taxable, exemptions, deductions, clubbing of income, and applicable tax rates.

Tax Year 2026-27 Sec 92–95 & 96–100 (2025) / Sec 56–65 (1961)

What is "Income from Other Sources"

It's the residuary, catch-all head of income. Any income that isn't excluded from total income under the Act, and isn't chargeable under Salaries, House Property, Business/Profession, or Capital Gains, falls here by default.

Under the 1961 Act this lived in Section 56. The Income-tax Act, 2025 restates the same principle in Section 92, within Chapter IV (Computation of Total Income).

RESIDUARY HEAD
Catch-all by design

If income doesn't fit Salaries, House Property, Business/Profession or Capital Gains, it defaults here.

Sec 92(2) — incomes specifically taxed here

Section 92(2) lists items chargeable under this head whenever they aren't already taxed under another head. Clause letters mirror the Act's own lettering (a)–(m).

92(2)(a)
Dividend

Any dividend received, taxed at slab rate.

92(2)(b)
Lottery, gambling & game winnings

Winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, races (incl. horse races), card games and games of any sort, or gambling/betting — taxed flat at 30%, no deductions.

92(2)(c)
Employees' fund contributions

Employees' PF/superannuation/ESI/welfare fund contributions received by the employer, if not taxed as business income.

92(2)(d)
Keyman insurance policy

Sums received (incl. bonus) under a Keyman insurance policy, if not taxed as business income or salary.

92(2)(e)
Interest on securities

If not taxed as business income.

92(2)(f)
Letting of machinery/plant/furniture

Hire income, if not taxed as business income.

92(2)(g)
Composite letting

Buildings let together with machinery/plant/furniture where inseparable, if not business income.

92(2)(h)
Forfeited advance money

Advance forfeited during negotiations for transfer of a capital asset, where the deal falls through.

92(2)(i)
Interest on enhanced compensation

50% standard deduction applies; no other deduction (see Sec 93(1)(f)).

92(2)(j)
Termination of employment

Compensation on termination of employment or modification of its terms.

92(2)(k)
Business trust distributions

Specified sums received by a unit holder, computed by the A−B−C formula in the Act.

92(2)(l)
Life insurance policy sums

Sums (incl. bonus) not otherwise exempt, to the extent exceeding premiums paid; excludes ULIPs.

Sec 92(2)(m) — three limbs, each with a ₹50,000 threshold

MONEY
Sum received without consideration

If the total for the year exceeds ₹50,000, the entire sum is taxable — not just the excess.

IMMOVABLE PROPERTY
Without / for inadequate consideration

Without consideration: taxable if stamp duty value > ₹50,000 (full value taxed). Inadequate consideration: taxable if (stamp duty value − consideration) exceeds the higher of ₹50,000 or 10% of consideration.

MOVABLE PROPERTY
Shares, jewellery, art, crypto, bullion

Covers shares/securities, jewellery, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, art, bullion, and virtual digital assets. Taxable if aggregate FMV received without consideration > ₹50,000, or FMV exceeds payment by > ₹50,000.

VALUATION TIMING
Agreement vs. registration date

Where dates differ, the stamp duty value on the agreement date applies — but only if part/full consideration was paid via banking/online channels on or before that date.

Sec 92(3) — exemptions from the gift provision

Sec 92(2)(m) does not apply to sums or property received in these situations:

Cl.Exempt situation
(a)From any relative — spouse, siblings, siblings of spouse/parents, lineal ascendants/descendants and their spouses; for an HUF, any member.
(b)On the occasion of the individual's marriage.
(c)Under a will or by way of inheritance.
(d)In contemplation of death of the payer/donor.
(e)From any local authority.
(f)From/by a registered non-profit organisation (with limited exceptions).
(g)Via transactions not regarded as "transfer" under Sec 70(1).
(h)From an individual, via a trust for the sole benefit of that individual's relative.
(i)Any other prescribed class of persons/conditions notified under the Rules.

SEC 94(4) — SPECIAL RULEFor winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, races, card games, other games, gambling or betting of any form — no deduction for any expenditure or allowance is allowed at all. Tax is on the gross winnings.

Sec 96–100 — clubbing of income

Anti-avoidance rules that stop you from lowering your tax bill by diverting income or assets to family members. Clubbed income keeps the character of its original head — so clubbed interest, dividends or rent typically still lands under Income from Other Sources, just in someone else's return.

Income diversion → Sec 96 Revocable transfer → Sec 97/98 Spouse/minor → Sec 99
SEC 96
Transfer of income, asset retained

If you transfer just the income from an asset while keeping ownership yourself, that income is still taxed in your hands as transferor — regardless of who receives it.

SEC 97 & 98
Revocable transfers

Where the transferor retains a power to reclaim the asset or its income (a "revocable transfer"), the income is clubbed back. Irrevocable transfers — e.g. a trust solely for a spouse's benefit that cannot be revoked during their lifetime — are excluded.

Sec 99 — situations where another's income is added to yours

Cl.Clubbing situation
(a)Spouse's remuneration from a concern in which you hold a substantial interest — clubbed, unless the spouse holds a technical/professional qualification and the income is solely attributable to applying that expertise.
(b)Income from assets transferred to your spouse without adequate consideration (except transfers under an agreement to live apart).
(c)Income from assets transferred to your son's wife without adequate consideration.
(d)Income from assets transferred to any third person for the immediate/deferred benefit of your spouse.
(e)Income from assets transferred to any third person for the benefit of your son's wife.
(f)All income of a minor child (under 18) — clubbed with whichever parent has the higher total income; stays with that parent in later years unless the AO is satisfied a change is warranted.
SUBSTANTIAL INTERESTBeneficial ownership of 20% or more of the voting power in a company, or a 20%-or-more share of profits in a firm/AOP.
NOT CLUBBED
Minor's own skill or talent income

Earnings from a minor's own manual work, skill, talent, specialised knowledge or experience (e.g. a child actor's or athlete's earnings).

NOT CLUBBED
Disabled minor's income

Income of a minor child with a disability of 40% or more (Sec 80U-equivalent) — assessed independently in the child's own hands.

Relief: the parent in whose hands a minor's income is clubbed can claim an exemption of ₹1,500 per minor child per year (or the actual clubbed income, if lower) — carried forward from the old Sec 10(32) exemption.

SEC 100The person whose income is actually clubbed into someone else's total income remains entitled to any TDS credit on that income, and remains jointly accountable for supplying information needed to assess it correctly.
